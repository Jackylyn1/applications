## Cover Letter Standards

Cover-letter structure only. Voice → `communication-rules.md`; fact source, language/register matching, relevance filter, email swap and PDF output → `application-standards.md`; positioning → `general-standards.md`.

The letter is prose in the LinkedIn-About register (`communication-rules.md`, *The person description stays prose*), but it connects her to this company's problem instead of describing her.

These rules define what every letter has to achieve, never its sentences or paragraph order. Never reuse a previous letter as a fill-in-the-blanks form.

### Format
- One page maximum (DIN A4).
- No enclosures line. Never end with "Anlage(n): …".

### What every letter must do
- **Don't retell the CV.** The CV lists what she did; the letter explains why that matters for this role. Cut any sentence that would work unchanged as a CV bullet.
- **Open strong.** The first paragraph earns the second: lead with the company's problem and her relevant answer to it, not with an announcement that she is applying.
- **Make the fit obvious early.** Why she suits this position is clear within the first few lines.
- **Establish a credible link** to the company or role: something specific and verifiable — their product, their domain, a technical problem they have. Never generic praise or a researched fact dropped in for effect.
- **Map requirements to evidence.** Connect the posting's important requirements to her most relevant experience, skills and results.
- **Impact over activity** → `general-standards.md`, *Name the outcome*. In prose, carry the "because" a bullet cannot.
- **Show competence and personality**, not only what she can do.
- **Never name individual side or private projects** — no repo names, no private or volunteer projects, no stacks. Keep the offer of work samples generic ("Arbeitsproben bringe ich gern mit").
- **Close forward-looking:** why she wants to contribute, plus a confident, non-pushy line about a personal conversation. Not flattery, and not a restatement of the opening.

### Default balance (guidance, not a fixed order)
- Roughly 40% the company and their problem, 40% how she solves it, 20% about her.
- A flow that usually works: (1) the company's problem or mission; (2) her experience against that need; (3) one or two concrete project examples; (4) the close. Deviate whenever a role reads better another way.
- Reference the specific company or posting in the introduction.

### Before it ships
- Proofread spelling, grammar and punctuation.
- Verify company name, recipient and exact job title against the posting.
