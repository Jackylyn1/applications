# Application Standards (CV & cover letter only)

Rules that apply **only** to job-application documents (CV and cover letter) —
NOT to the LinkedIn profile or social media. Inherits the universal branding
(`general-standards.md`) and voice (`communication-rules.md`). Source:
`Application_Standards_and_Career_Guidelines.pdf`.

## Fact source
- Never-invent and the `profile.json`-only rule are universal → `general-standards.md`, *Evidence over claims*. Nothing document-specific to add here.

## Language & register (hard)
- Write the document in the **same language as the job offer**.
- Mirror the posting's **register** — "du" or "Sie" — while staying professional.

## Project Description Standard (experience & project bullets)
This is a **pool to select from, never a checklist to work through** — and it decides *what* a bullet is about, never how it is written. Bullet form (`Topic: outcome`, one claim, one line, no filler, numbers where they exist) is universal and defined once in `communication-rules.md`, *Bullet points*.

Rank these by what the posting asks for and keep only what earns its line:
1. Measurable impact — **first choice whenever a number exists in `profile.json`.**
2. The capability or component delivered, named with its established term.
3. Optimizations and the engineering decisions behind them.
4. Technologies — only where the term is what the reader searches for; a stack list is not a bullet.
5. Transferable technical and leadership skills.

Problem, responsibility and challenges are **context for choosing the bullet, not content for it.** They explain to the writer why the bullet matters; spelled out in the text they become the "because" that bullets must not carry (`communication-rules.md`, *Writing style*), and "responsibility" phrasing in particular reintroduces the duty-oriented wording the bullet rule forbids. Carry them into the cover letter or the interview instead.

## Relevance filter (both documents)
- Include only what supports her suitability for **this** role — everything else, however interesting, stays out. Name technologies only where they are relevant to the posting. In the CV this drives which bullets are surfaced, reordered or dropped (`cv-standards.md`); in the letter it decides which experience is worth a sentence.

## Keywords / ATS
- Mirror the posting's exact terminology (including German/English variants of the same tech) wherever it is genuinely true — embedded naturally, never keyword-stuffed. A recruiter should see the fit in ~5 seconds and the ATS must not filter her out.

## Four questions every application should answer
1. What kinds of problems do I enjoy solving?
2. How do I approach unfamiliar technologies?
3. What evidence demonstrates how I work?
4. Why does that make me valuable for this specific role?
