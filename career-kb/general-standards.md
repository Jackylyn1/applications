# Universal Professional Branding Standards

The shared foundation for **every** channel — CV, cover letter, LinkedIn profile
and social media. Hard rules; they override style preferences.

Where the other rules live (referenced, never duplicated here):
- Voice / tone / phrasing → `communication-rules.md`
- CV & cover-letter-only rules → `application-standards.md`
- Channel specifics → `linkedin-standards.md`, `social-media-standards.md`
- CV build/layout → `cv-standards.md`; cover-letter structure → `cover-letter-standards.md`

Sources: `Application_Standards_and_Career_Guidelines.pdf`, `Professional_Branding_Standards.pdf` (Group A), `LinkedIn_Profile_Standards.pdf` (its positioning, evidence, philosophy and AI sections are universal, not LinkedIn-specific).

## Positioning (project this on every channel)
- Present Jacqueline as an **experienced software engineer with growing architectural expertise**.
- Show an engineer who **understands systems end-to-end** and **bridges business and technology**.
- Show **strategic thinking, not just implementation** — architecture before implementation.
- Headline positioning: **"Software Engineer with Architecture and AI Focus."**
- Target roles: AI Software Developer, AI Consultant, Solution Architect, Software Architect (PHP Developer as a strong baseline).
- Core differentiator: **designs systems rather than merely implementing features** — measurable optimization, architecture decisions, AI integration, performance engineering, continuous improvement.
- **Never position her as backend-only** (Jacqueline, 2026-07-28). Backend is the deepest area, not the boundary: Python, TypeScript/frontend and containers/CI are part of the profile and belong in the **first visible lines** of every channel, not in a technology list further down. "Backend-Software" as an opening self-description is wrong.

## AI positioning (every channel) — the primary focus
- **AI is the main focus of the profile** and has to be discoverable as such (Jacqueline, 2026-07-28): it belongs in the first visible line of every channel, in role titles where it is genuinely true, and in the search terms she wants to be found under — not on position three of a list.
- It is a focus, **not a separate identity**: AI is shown *through* engineering work, never as a buzzword or trend topic. Both halves of this rule are load-bearing — the fix for "too little AI" is more AI *evidence*, never more AI *claims*.
- What to show: she knows **where AI creates measurable value**, **evaluates tools critically**, **optimises workflows** with them, **learns fast**, and **integrates AI into software engineering**.
- **Never reduce her AI work to "just a development tool"** (Jacqueline, 2026-07-28). Formulations like "LLMs are a tool for me, not a topic of their own" undersell it as throwing one or two prompts at a problem. What is actually there and has to be visible: agent workflows, subagents, MCP, deterministic verification of AI findings, prompt/context/token optimisation, model comparison with documented cost-vs-quality trade-offs, and LLM observability/tracing.

## Evidence over claims (hard)
- **Never claim a skill — demonstrate it** through projects, achievements, metrics, architecture decisions, technical examples and lessons learned. **Show, don't tell.**
- **Never invent.** Use only facts from `profile.json`. Never invent skills, tools, experience, dates, numbers or metrics for any channel — CV, cover letter, LinkedIn or social media. If something is missing, **propose a formulation and ask**; do not fill the gap (Jacqueline, 2026-07-28). If a posting wants something absent from the KB, map it to a genuine adjacent skill or — only if it is in the role's `must_learn` — present it honestly as "currently strengthening".

## Never upgrade the role she had (hard)
Jacqueline, 2026-07-29, after "Softwarearchitektur" ended up in her Gastro IT job title although she did little architecture work there. Overstatement rarely enters as an invented fact — it enters as a **stronger word for a real one**, and it survives review because the underlying activity is genuine.
- **Keep the KB's verb.** "Used" does not become "introduced", "worked on" does not become "built", "contributed to" does not become "designed", "supported" does not become "led". If the KB's verb feels weak, that is information, not a formatting problem — the fix is asking her, never a synonym.
- **A competence may headline an entry only if that entry's own evidence carries it.** A field like architecture, consulting or performance engineering belongs in a job title, a headline or a top-billed skill only when the work named *under that entry* demonstrates it. Otherwise it stays where its evidence is — one specific component, one project, one volunteer role.
- **Name the component, not the discipline.** "Real-time pivot-event synchronisation" and "multi-tenant REST API" are hers; "Softwarearchitektur" as a summary of the same work claims the whole field. The component is both more honest and more precise (see `communication-rules.md`, *Use the established technical term*), and it needs no disclaimer to be correctly scoped.
- **Use the plain role noun — no elevating prefix** (Jacqueline, 2026-07-30: "Kernentwicklerin ist irrelevant, nimm Entwicklerin"). "Kern-", "Lead", "Senior", "Principal" and "Haupt-" are claims about rank, not descriptions of work, and the work below the title has to carry them. Plain "Entwicklerin" / "Software Engineer" is both honest and stronger — the components named underneath do the ranking.
- **Team size stays out** (Jacqueline, 2026-07-30: "die Anzahl der Teammitglieder kann raus"). How many people were in the team says nothing about what she contributed, and a small number invites the wrong inference while a large one implies a share she did not have. `profile.json` keeps the real figure (4 developers at Gastro IT) so no text can *overstate* it — but the default is to omit it and name her components instead. Team size she *coordinated* is different: that is her own scope of responsibility and may be stated (~15 at Narutorpg.de).
- **Drop scope adjectives in front of the product.** "Kassensystem", not "verteiltes Kassensystem" (Jacqueline, 2026-07-30). The distributed nature belongs in the bullet that shows it — cloud back-office, on-premise servers, mobile terminals — where it is evidence rather than an adjective.
- **Tools she used are not achievements she owns.** Naming a framework, a pipeline or an observability stack is fine; implying she chose, built or introduced it is not, unless the KB says so.
- **Flag, do not soften silently.** When a claim has to be reduced, say what was reduced and why in the output's notes, and add the question that would restore the stronger version if the answer supports it. A quietly weakened profile loses evidence she may genuinely have.

## Name the outcome, not just the activity (hard)
- Every achievement states **what it changed**, not only what she did. "Built X" is half a sentence; "built X, which did Y" is the whole one. Prefer **measurable improvement over trends**, and **measurable business value** over an activity list.
- This applies especially to **changes she initiated** rather than was assigned (introducing AI-assisted development at Gastro IT, introducing automated testing and the starter-kit optimisation at wpt-online): the initiative is only half the story — the effect is the evidence.
- Where a real number exists in `profile.json`, use it. Where none exists, describe the effect qualitatively and **ask for the number** — never estimate one (see *Evidence over claims*).

## Engineering philosophy to communicate
- Systems thinking; optimisation mindset; evidence-based decisions; continuous improvement and iteration; architecture before implementation; business understanding; measuring outcomes.
- **Automation matters to her** and should be visible as a through-line, not as a claim (Jacqueline, 2026-07-28): she automates what is repetitive and error-prone — test automation, CI pipelines, AI-assisted code audits, reproducible container environments, data pipelines. Show it through the things she actually automated.

## Target impression (every channel)
- "This person is technically strong, analytical, trustworthy and understands software engineering beyond writing code."
- And explicitly **not** "someone chasing the latest trend" — the move toward AI and architecture has to read as a natural next step from what she already does.
