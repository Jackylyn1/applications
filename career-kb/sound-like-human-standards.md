## Sound_Like_Human_Standards

Sources: `Human_Writing_Ruleset.pdf` ([applicant], 2026-07-30);
`LinkedIn_Post_Writing_Rules_Human_First.pdf` ([applicant], 2026-08-09 — restates these rules for posts
and adds the two below; its channel-specific half lives in `social-media-standards.md`).
Governs **voice** in every channel, so it sits next to `communication-rules.md` rather than in any channel file. Where a rule here already exists there, that file stays the authority — this one is the source of record and adds the concrete blacklists.

### Core principle
Write like an experienced human with a clear purpose. Authenticity, specificity and a consistent personal voice beat polish. A single AI tell is survivable; an accumulation of predictable AI patterns is what discredits the text.

### Must do
- Get to the point immediately.
- Use concrete examples, names, numbers, dates, evidence.
- Vary sentence length, paragraph length, rhythm and structure — and vary sentence **openings** and punctuation too.
- Contractions and natural conversational language where the register allows it.
- Active voice, everyday words.
- Make genuine judgments instead of forced neutrality.
- Trust the reader: no over-explaining an obvious conclusion. This is about conclusions, not
  vocabulary — it never licenses specialist jargon the audience does not share (per-channel
  audience → the channel file).
- Let paragraphs flow instead of bolting on transitions.
- Edit aggressively for repetition and filler.
- Finish naturally — no formulaic conclusion.
- Allow small natural imperfections and an individual voice.
- Observations and real examples instead of abstractions.
- **Keep her certainty level.** "Ich glaube" stays tentative, "mir ist aufgefallen" never becomes
  "das beweist", and one experience never becomes a universal rule. Never make her sound more
  authoritative, senior or certain than the evidence carries.

### Must not do
- **Buzzwords**: delve, underscore, pivotal, transformative, seamless, robust, leverage, unlock, empower, elevate, navigate, tapestry, landscape.
- **Stock phrases**: "It's important to note", "At its core", "In conclusion", "Overall", "Here's why", "But here's the thing", "The result?".
- **Hedging** on every statement — "generally", "typically", "tends to" and relatives.
- Manufactured suspense, conflict or profundity where none exists.
- Repetitive LinkedIn-style cadence, or one-line paragraphs throughout.
- Overused em dashes, rhetorical questions, Rule-of-Three lists.
- Bullets, frameworks and checklists where prose works better. (Where bullets *are* the right shape — CV/LinkedIn work entries — `communication-rules.md`, *Bullet points*, governs them.)
- Repeating the prompt, summarising the obvious, padding intro or conclusion.
- Hype without justification: revolutionary, groundbreaking, game-changing.
- Generic opening templates ("In today's fast-paced world…").
- Repeating the same sentence structure or paragraph length.
- Sounding performative, inspirational or universally agreeable instead of specific and purposeful.

### Editing checklist
Never "improve" a text by making it less funny, less personal or less recognisable — polish is the
first thing to give up, not the voice.

**A rework changes voice, never facts.** When an existing text is rewritten for tone, every number,
name, price, duration and technical detail carries over unchanged. Nothing gets rounded, softened or
re-derived on the way. Where a fact has to go, it goes whole. A voice edit is where honest numbers
quietly drift, so treat any changed figure in a rewrite as a bug.

Strip repeated buzzwords, repetitive transitions, excessive em dashes, AI-style formulas, generic filler, repeated sentence openings, unnecessary conclusions and unsupported hype. Replace abstractions with concrete detail. Final test: does the piece read as one identifiable human author, or as a generic assistant?
